Part I: Humany Meowerese

Humany Meowerese is an extension of English that adds a couple of catsy words.
NOTE: I might deprecate Humany Meowerese sometime in the future as it's basically just slang.

Major Vocabulary

Meow

Meow in Humany Meowerese has some extra uses as a noun (i.e. to replace swears), an adjective to put emphasis on words (i.e. this cat is so meowing cuddly), or a sentence ender (I feel sleepy, meow.).

Other Vocabulary

Meowity meow - general exclamation
Meowing meow - slightly swear-y meow
Meowing meows - strong swear
Purry - happy, satisfied
Hissy - angered, agitated
Tailwaggy/Tail-Waggy - happy or nervous

Part II: Catsy Meowerese

Catsy Meowerese is a language of meows encoded in English. Meowerese V3 adds onto Meowerese V2 by adding actual words, but the newly renamed "Feeling meows" (with the exception of mrrm being deprecated and hiss being added) are the same and thus backwards compatible of sorts. I personally prefer to use mrow over meow when using Catsy Meowerese to differentiate it from Humany Meowerese.

General grammar (yes this exists now)

Verbs and nyowns (see what i did there again?)
To form a word in Meowerese, take a concept (or some general meow) and a modifier meow and mash them together, like so:
Mrowmmrowm + mrowrrow = Mrowmmrowm mrowrrow = food (food+noun)
Mrowmmrowm + mreew = Mrowmmrowm mreew = to eat
You can also mash a concept, mrowrrow (noun-ifier) and mreew (verb-ifier) to make doer of verb. Example:
Mrrmmrrm + mreew + mrowrrow = Mrrmmrrm mreew mrowrrow = someone who is sleeping
Consult the dictionary at the bottom for a list of the less obvious words formed in this manner.

Subjects, objects, and possessive pronouns
In Meowerese, subjects and objects use the same word. For example, mew can mean I, me, and my.

Negating
The meow mra is used as a prefix to make negative words. For example:
Mew mra mrrmrrm mreew - I am not sleeping.

Sentences
Sentences can be formed in many ways. This section covers normal sentences, while the next section covers questions. Here are some general structures:
[subject] [noun] - [subject] is [noun]
[noun] [subject] - [subject]'s [noun]
[subject] [feeling] mrowrrow - [subject] is feeling [feeling]
[subject] [verb] - [subject] is [verb]ing
[subject] [verb] [object] - [subject] is [verb]ing [object]
[subject] [feeling] mreew [object] - [subject] is making [object] feel [feeling]
[concept] - I want [concept]
[feeling] - I feel [feeling] (exclamatory)
[noun] - There is [noun]
[verb] - Do [verb] (imperative intransitive form)
[verb] [object] - Do [verb] to [object] (imperative transitive form)

Questions
Questions in Meowerese are generally formed by putting a question mark at the end of a sentence. For example:
Mewa mrrmmrrm mreew? - Are you sleeping?

However, there is one special exception:
[subject] [noun]? - [subject] is [noun]?
[noun]? [subject]. - [noun] is [subject]'s?
For that last sentence, [noun] is pronounced with the pitch rise instead of [subject].

Advanced Grammar

*Saying I like or I hate, etc.
In Meowerese, you can say I [like, hate, etc.] something by saying something make you [happy, sad, etc.]. For example:
Mewa mrrow mreew mew - You make me happy/I like you
Mewa mraaa mreew mew - You make me feel silly/I think you are funny

Writing Meowerese

Meowerese is written with the Latin alphabet. Spaces are used between sentences, and can also be used between words for more legibility. For punctuation, the question mark must be used if asking a question, and periods must be used unless the divide between sentences is clear. (i.e. writing on a new line every sentence, or writing only one sentence.)

Tweakable meows

Normally, meows have a fixed length on the syllables, but some meows allow for changing of the syllable lengths for greater emphasis. They will be listed below.
Mrrow - more r's can be added for more happy
Mroww - more w's can be added for more sad
Mraaa - more a's can be added for more silly/relaxed
